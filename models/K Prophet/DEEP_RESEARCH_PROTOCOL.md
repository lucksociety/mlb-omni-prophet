# DEEP RESEARCH PROTOCOL
## The SP Strikeout Research Workflow
### Mandatory Protocol for Pitcher Prop Modeling

This protocol has been recalibrated specifically for Starting Pitcher (SP) Strikeout (K Prophet) Predictions. The focus shifts from general scoring to the specific intersection of pitcher "stuff," batter discipline, and external variables that influence the K-rate.

---

### 1. Opponent Discipline & Lineup Composition

- **Whiff & Chase Rate Audit**: Analyze the projected starting lineup’s K% and SwStr% (Swinging Strike Percentage) over the last 15 days. Identify "high-frequency swingers" vs. "disciplined contact hitters."
- **Splits vs. Pitch Type**: Cross-reference the SP’s primary K-pitch (e.g., Slider, Sweeper, Curveball) against the lineup’s vSL or vCB (Pitch Value) metrics.
- **Lineup Integrity**: Confirm the presence of "Strikeout Anchors"—players who historically struggle with high velocity or specific break—and check for late-minute substitutions of high-contact bench players.

### 2. Pitcher "Stuff" & Command Audit

- **Velocity & Spin Consistency**: Review the SP’s last three starts for any dip in average fastball velocity or spin rate (RPM). A drop in velocity often leads to a decrease in "swing and miss" capability.
- **CSW% Tracking**: Evaluate the pitcher’s recent Called Strikes + Whiffs (CSW%). This is the most predictive metric for K-potential, focusing on the pitcher's ability to freeze batters and induce misses.
- **Strike Zone Heat Maps**: Analyze the SP’s ability to "tunnel" pitches. Are they hitting the edges, or are they leaking balls into the heart of the plate (leading to contact rather than Ks)?

### 3. Environmental & Umpire Variables

- **Umpire Scouting**: This is critical. Identify the home plate umpire’s K-Zone Factor. A "pitcher-friendly" umpire with a wide or low zone can increase K-projections by 10%−15%.
- **The "Batter’s Eye" & Lighting**: Adjust for stadiums with notorious "batter's eyes" or specific lighting conditions (e.g., late afternoon shadows at Dodger Stadium) that make pitch recognition difficult.
- **Weather Impact**: High humidity can increase "grip" and movement on breaking balls, while cold temperatures can affect finger sensitivity and command of "feel" pitches like changeups.

### 4. Game Script & Volume Projections

- **Pitch Count & Hook Logic**: Analyze the manager’s "leash." Does the pitcher have a hard cap at 85–95 pitches, or are they allowed to go deep? Verify if the bullpen is rested; a gassed bullpen forces the SP to stay in longer, increasing K-opportunity.
- **Third Time Through the Order (TTTO)**: Evaluate the SP’s efficiency drop-off. If a pitcher's K-rate plummets during the third pass, calculate the probability of them being pulled before the 6th inning.
- **Fatigue Multipliers**: Factor in the "travel tax." A pitcher starting the first game of a series after a cross-country flight may have diminished "snap" on their breaking stuff.

---

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Probability Distribution Requirement**
> All projections must be expressed as a probability distribution (e.g., Over/Under 6.5 Ks) rather than a flat number, accounting for the standard deviation of the SP’s recent performance.
